# 1. PRODUCT VISION

We want to build an AI-powered mental-health follow-up platform connecting patients with their psychiatrists between clinical consultations.

The idea is to extend the relationship between a psychiatrist and a patient beyond their occasional appointments.

Imagine the following situation:

A person is already being followed by a psychiatrist. During a consultation, the psychiatrist suggests that the patient use our platform to communicate regularly with an AI companion between appointments.

The psychiatrist creates a patient profile and provides relevant context about the individual.

This context could include:

- General information relevant to the patient's follow-up.
- Difficulties or symptoms currently being monitored.
- The psychiatrist's observations.
- Areas of concern.
- Therapeutic objectives.
- Particular indicators that should be monitored.
- Activities or exercises recommended by the psychiatrist.
- Other relevant information that helps interpret the patient's conversations.

The patient then interacts with an AI companion through our platform, potentially on a daily basis.

The AI provides supportive conversations, encourages reflection and may suggest appropriate activities.

Meanwhile, Sentinel analyzes the conversations over time.

It extracts relevant mental-health signals, tracks their evolution and provides the psychiatrist with structured reports and alerts when concerning situations emerge.

**The central idea is to transform everyday conversations into structured, contextualized and longitudinal insights that help psychiatrists better understand their patients between consultations.**

This is NOT intended to replace psychiatrists, diagnose mental disorders or make autonomous clinical decisions.

The psychiatrist remains responsible for clinical assessment and care.

# 2. THE THREE MAIN COMPONENTS OF THE PLATFORM

The product has three main components.

## A. Psychiatrist Interface

The psychiatrist should be able to:

1. Create a patient profile.
2. Enter or upload information about the patient.
3. Define monitoring objectives.
4. Specify particular indicators they want Sentinel to follow.
5. Configure therapeutic objectives and recommended activities.
6. Review patient conversations or relevant excerpts, according to the consent and access model.
7. Receive daily or weekly reports.
8. Receive alerts when predefined concerning situations emerge.
9. Visualize the evolution of monitored indicators over time.
10. Review the evidence behind every AI-generated observation.

The psychiatrist-provided context is particularly important.

I do NOT want it to be treated merely as a long prompt sent to the chatbot.

Ideally, it should be a structured component of the system, stored in the database and accessible to the relevant analytical components.

For example, a psychiatrist might create this patient context:

"Patient is experiencing depressive symptoms, social isolation and difficulties maintaining daily routines. We want to monitor emotional distress, sleep-related difficulties, social interactions and expressions of hopelessness. The patient is also working on gradually increasing social interactions."

The system should use this context to interpret conversations and compare observations with the patient's individual monitoring objectives.

The context should ideally be versioned, so that changes made by the psychiatrist can be tracked over time.

## B. Patient Interface

The patient interacts with an AI companion.

The AI should:

- Encourage the patient to express their feelings.
- Ask appropriate follow-up questions.
- Help the patient reflect on their day.
- Encourage healthy routines.
- Suggest simple and appropriate activities.
- Support the patient's therapeutic objectives.
- Maintain a supportive, empathetic and non-judgmental tone.

Example:

Patient:
"I didn't leave my room today. I didn't feel like talking to anyone."

AI:
"I'm sorry today was difficult. Has this been happening frequently recently, or was today particularly difficult?"

The AI should not attempt to diagnose the patient or independently prescribe treatments.

Activity suggestions should respect the psychiatrist's instructions and predefined safety boundaries.

There should be a distinction between:

- Activities explicitly recommended or approved by the psychiatrist.
- General, low-risk supportive suggestions that the AI can make independently within predefined boundaries.

## C. Intelligence and Monitoring Layer

This is the technical heart of Sentinel.

The platform should analyze patient conversations and transform them into structured observations.

There are two levels of analysis.

### Level 1: Individual Message and Conversation Analysis

We want to extract multiple signals from conversations.

A single message can contain several signals simultaneously.

For example:

"I haven't been sleeping, I don't want to see my friends anymore, and I feel completely useless."

Potential observations:

- Sleep difficulties.
- Social isolation.
- Worthlessness.
- Emotional distress.

This is why I am interested in a **multi-label classification approach**, rather than a simple single-class classifier.

Potential categories include:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Emotional distress | Sadness, anxiety, anger or other difficult emotional states. |
| Hopelessness | Expressions of hopelessness or helplessness. |
| Social isolation | Withdrawal from social interactions. |
| Sleep difficulties | Reported sleep problems or irregularities. |
| Motivation | Changes in motivation or interest. |
| Worthlessness | Negative self-perception or feelings of worthlessness. |
| Suicidal ideation | Expressions of suicidal thoughts. |
| Suicidal intent | Statements potentially indicating intent. |
| Planning signals | Statements potentially indicating preparation or planning. |
| Protective factors | Support systems, positive activities and reasons for living. |

These are linguistic observations, not clinical diagnoses.

The model should also distinguish:

- First-person disclosures.
- Third-person descriptions.
- Academic discussions.
- Fictional conversations.
- Quoted statements.
- Negation.
- Ambiguous expressions.

For example, a patient discussing suicide in an academic context should not automatically trigger the same interpretation as someone describing personal suicidal thoughts.

The system should preserve uncertainty rather than force every message into a definitive category.

### Level 2: Longitudinal Analysis

This is one of the most important aspects of our product.

We do not want to analyze only the latest message.

We want to understand how the patient's situation evolves across days and weeks.

For example:

| Indicator | Monday | Wednesday | Friday | Evolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional distress | Moderate | High | High | Increasing |
| Social isolation | Low | Moderate | High | Increasing |
| Motivation | High | Moderate | Low | Decreasing |
| Hopelessness | None detected | Mild | Moderate | Emerging |

The platform should identify:

- Recurring patterns.
- New signals.
- Increasing or decreasing indicators.
- Significant changes compared with previous periods.
- Potential improvements.
- Contradictions between observations.
- Differences between the patient's current situation and their initial context.

An important example:

A patient initially reports that they are feeling better. However, over the following three weeks, their conversations increasingly mention social withdrawal and loss of motivation.

The platform should identify this evolution and make it visible to the psychiatrist.

These trends are monitoring indicators, not clinically validated predictions of suicide risk or deterioration.

# 3. PROPOSED AI ARCHITECTURE: MULTI-AGENT SYSTEM

I am interested in a multi-agent architecture.

However, I do not want to introduce agents simply for the sake of having many agents.

Each component should have a clear responsibility and produce structured outputs.

Here is the architecture I have in mind. You are free to simplify or improve it.

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Patient Context Agent | Understands the psychiatrist-provided context and monitoring objectives. |
| Emotion Analysis Agent | Identifies emotional states and their evolution. |
| Mental Health Signals Agent | Detects relevant signals such as hopelessness, isolation, worthlessness and suicidal ideation. |
| Behavioral Analysis Agent | Monitors reported sleep, motivation, routines and social interactions. |
| Temporal Analysis Agent | Compares observations across conversations and identifies changes over time. |
| Activity Recommendation Agent | Suggests suitable activities within psychiatrist-defined boundaries. |
| Safety Agent | Identifies potentially urgent situations and activates predefined safety procedures. |
| Judge Agent | Aggregates findings, handles disagreement and produces reports or alerts. |

### Important architectural preference

I do not necessarily expect eight independent LLMs.

I would prefer a practical architecture where several analytical components share the same underlying NLP model.

For example:

- A shared pretrained NLP model extracts relevant signals.
- Python modules process temporal information.
- Structured rules identify predefined safety conditions.
- A Judge component aggregates the results.

The Judge does not necessarily need to be an LLM. A deterministic Python implementation may be more reliable and achievable for the MVP.

A potentially concerning safety signal must never be dismissed simply because other components disagree.

Every important output should ideally include:

- The observation.
- The category.
- The source message.
- The timestamp.
- The confidence or uncertainty.
- The relevant patient context.
- The reason why the observation matters.

# 4. MODEL STRATEGY

I am particularly interested in using a pretrained model for mental-health-related text classification.

Some models and approaches worth investigating:

- MentalBERT.
- MentalRoBERTa.
- RoBERTa-base.
- Other publicly available mental-health text classification checkpoints.

I previously considered using a multi-label classifier with a shared encoder and a classification head.

The idea is to obtain several probabilities for each message rather than one exclusive class.

For example:

```python
{
    "emotional_distress": 0.87,
    "social_isolation": 0.92,
    "hopelessness": 0.65,
    "suicidal_ideation": 0.12,
    "protective_factor": 0.05
}
```

These numbers are illustrative, not calibrated clinical probabilities.

I want you to investigate whether we should:

1. Use an existing fine-tuned classifier.
2. Use a pretrained encoder and fine-tune it.
3. Start with a simpler model or hybrid NLP approach.
4. Combine a classifier with deterministic rules.

We have only 36 hours, so I do not want to spend most of the hackathon training a model.

Please consider:

- Whether model checkpoints are actually available.
- Their licenses and access restrictions.
- Whether they are pretrained encoders or already fine-tuned classifiers.
- CPU inference performance.
- Whether fine-tuning is realistic with the available data.
- Whether the available datasets match our proposed labels.
- The time required to integrate and evaluate each option.

Potential datasets to investigate include publicly available research resources such as CLPsych shared-task datasets, UMD Reddit Suicidality Dataset and Dreaddit.

Check their availability, licensing and suitability before recommending them.

Do not assume that subreddit membership or synthetic labels represent clinical ground truth.

For the hackathon, synthetic conversations can be used to demonstrate longitudinal behavior, but we must distinguish demonstration results from actual model validation.

We have no paid AI APIs or dedicated hackathon compute infrastructure.

Prefer open-source tools and local inference.

# 5. MEMORY AND PATIENT HISTORY

The system needs to retain information across conversations.

I want you to consider a memory architecture with three levels:

### Episodic memory

Stores the actual conversation events:

- Patient identifier.
- Session identifier.
- Message identifier.
- Timestamp.
- Speaker.
- Message content.

### Semantic memory

Stores structured observations extracted from conversations:

- Observation category.
- Evidence.
- Source message identifier.
- Timestamp.
- Confidence or uncertainty.
- Context.
- Relevant monitoring objective.

### Temporal memory

Stores information about evolution:

- Recurrence of observations.
- Changes in indicators.
- Previous reports.
- Previous alerts.
- Follow-up history.

For the MVP, SQLite should probably be sufficient.

A vector database is optional. Do not introduce one unless you can justify its necessity.

The system should retrieve relevant historical information rather than repeatedly process the entire conversation history.

# 6. WHAT THE PSYCHIATRIST SHOULD RECEIVE

The intelligence layer should produce three main outputs.

## A. Daily Reports

Example:

**Patient 001 — Daily Report**

- Emotional state: Predominantly negative.
- Main observations: Reported social isolation and reduced motivation.
- Sleep: Mentioned difficulties sleeping.
- Positive observations: Mentioned speaking with a family member.
- Change compared with previous days: Increased expressions of isolation.
- Safety assessment: No predefined urgent signal detected.
- Suggested follow-up: Review the evolution of social withdrawal during the next consultation.

The psychiatrist should be able to inspect the original evidence behind each observation.

Reports should distinguish what the patient explicitly said from what the system inferred.

## B. Alerts

Alerts should be generated when predefined concerning conditions emerge.

Examples:

- New expressions of suicidal ideation.
- Possible indications of intent or planning.
- Significant changes in several monitored indicators.
- A concerning combination of observations.

The platform should distinguish levels of urgency and provide the evidence behind the alert.

For the prototype, we can simulate notifications inside the psychiatrist dashboard.

We should not claim that a real psychiatrist has been contacted unless an actual staffed response process exists.

## C. Evolution Dashboard

The psychiatrist should be able to visualize indicators over time.

Potential visualizations:

- Emotional distress over the last 30 days.
- Social isolation over the last 30 days.
- Motivation over the last 30 days.
- Sleep difficulties over the last 30 days.
- Changes in reported protective factors.
- Timeline of important observations and alerts.

The dashboard should make it easy to understand what has changed since the last consultation.

# 7. ACTIVITY RECOMMENDATIONS

Another important aspect of the product is that it should not only identify concerning signals.

It should also help patients engage in activities that support their therapeutic objectives.

Examples:

| Observation | Possible supportive activity |
|---|---|
| Reduced motivation | Suggest a small, achievable daily task. |
| Social isolation | Suggest contacting someone they trust. |
| Irregular routines | Suggest establishing a consistent daily routine. |
| Reported stress | Suggest a simple relaxation exercise. |
| Reduced physical activity | Suggest a short walk if appropriate. |

Recommendations should be contextualized using the psychiatrist's instructions.

The system should never independently prescribe treatments or modify clinical objectives.

For the MVP, simple predefined recommendation rules may be enough.

# 8. THE END-TO-END PRODUCT WORKFLOW

Here is the complete workflow I envision:

1. A psychiatrist creates a patient profile.
2. The psychiatrist enters the patient's context.
3. The psychiatrist defines individual monitoring objectives.
4. The patient accesses their conversational AI interface.
5. The patient starts interacting with the AI.
6. Messages are stored with timestamps and session identifiers.
7. The NLP system extracts relevant multi-label observations.
8. The system stores these observations in structured memory.
9. Historical observations are retrieved.
10. The Temporal Analysis component identifies changes over time.
11. The Safety component checks predefined safety conditions.
12. The Judge aggregates the outputs.
13. The system generates a daily report.
14. If appropriate, an alert is created.
15. The psychiatrist reviews the patient dashboard.
16. The psychiatrist can acknowledge an alert and record follow-up actions.

This is the complete product vision, not necessarily the implementation scope for the hackathon.

# 11. IMPORTANT SAFETY AND PRIVACY CONSIDERATIONS

This is a mental-health-related product, so safety must influence the architecture.

The platform must:

- Avoid presenting AI outputs as medical diagnoses.
- Distinguish observations from clinical conclusions.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Provide traceable evidence for important findings.
- Avoid claiming to predict suicide with certainty.
- Ensure urgent signals are not suppressed by majority voting.
- Define what happens when an urgent signal is detected.
- Keep a human professional responsible for clinical decisions.

For the hackathon, use synthetic patient data exclusively.

A real deployment would require appropriate patient consent, GDPR-compliant processing, secure access controls, data-retention policies, clinical safety assessment and a reliable human escalation process.
